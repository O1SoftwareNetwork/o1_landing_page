O(1) Business Objectives (The Problem)

At O(1) Software Network, we're solving the problem of education.

There are many ways to learn a new skill or level-up professionally, including 4-year degrees, software engineering bootcamps, online tutorials, and self-education, and with each of these options, there lay pros and cons. Our goal is to develop a solution which adopts all of the pros while eliminating all of the cons.

4-Year Degree

Here at O(1) Software Network, we believe that a 4-year degree is among the greatest of options when developing professionally and learning new skills. Along with the pros, there are also some pretty solid cons too.

Pros

  • A highly structured approach.
  • Opportunities to network and connect with others whos share your interests and future profession.
  • A full education in your path of choice.
  • A bachelor's degree which places you at the top of the pile for many companies who require this.

Cons

  • You'll be taking classes that you never end up using which cost you time and money.
  • The overall cost is typically very high.
  • It takes four years, so despite your ability to learn fast, there are limits to your growth if you solely follow the path created for you.
  • The technology industry changes so rapidly, by the time you finish the 4-year degree, a lot of the subjects, technologies, and methods you learn will be outdated. Concepts typically last decades or longer, so the theory is great.

Software Engineering Bootcamp

Software engineering bootcamps solve the problem of time and money while sacrificing some other things.

Pros

  • A structured approach
  • Opportunities to network and connect with others who share your interests and future profession.
  • Focus on in-demand technologies.
  • Typically receive a certificate which is not as great as a degree for employers, but better than nothing.

Cons

  • Less people to network with than the 4-year education path because you have a smaller curriculum and less classes.
  • Lack of theory and computer science education
  • Surface-level education in a lot of technologies instead of deep dives into important ones.

Online Tutorials

Online tutorials are great, but we all know about tutorial hell. The goal with these is to take one, modify it to increase retention and learning building your own version of what they're telling you to build, then move onto documentation and building projects, but this doesn't always happen.

Pros

  • Very quick way to learn concepts.
  • You could move at your own pace.
  • Accessible to everybody for very low prices or free altogether.
  • The best people in the industry teaching you concepts.
  • You can focus only on the technologies YOU want to learna and focus on.

Cons

  • Less engaging because many times, the knowledge being taught is either too difficult or too easy, and finding this sweet-spot with prerecorded videos is difficult.
  • Very little structure
  • Some tutorial creators have communities you can join to network, but most of them don't so there are less opportunities to network and connect with others who share your interests and are pursuing a similar path as you.
  • Most of the time you're building somebody elses project instead of building your own.

Self-taught

Self-taught is a path for many, but in many ways the most challenging to pursue for many reasons as well.

Pros

  • You can create the exact curriculum you want and take the path you want to take.
  • You could move at your own pace.
Cons
  • Many times the path you want to take is not in-demand or a valuable path. It is easy to waste time going down rabbit hole learning things you will never use. You get better at this as you continue to self-teach, but in the beginning, it could get bad.
  • It is the most difficult path to connect with others and network professionally because if you want to do this, you need to figure out how to do it on your own.
  • This path begins with zero structure, and you need to create the structure on your own.

O(1) Software Network HOSTED Software Engineering Programs

At O(1) Software Network, we build projects. And these projects are open to the world to work on. Whether you're an experienced software engineer who is passionate about a project or concept we're working to solve or a new, aspiring software engineer, we have something for you.

Experienced Developers

Each one of our projects are well-documented and easy to get involved in. Just check out the GitHub repository for the project, read the readme.md, and get involved. Everything you need to know is right there.

New, Aspiring Software Engineers

For people who are new to software development or just want to master the concepts found in large-scale organizations, we have weekly workshops where members can sign up, ask questions, select an issue or task, write some code, exchange ideas, connect with others and so much more. We're always working on real-world projects solving real-world problems, so everybody has the opportunity to learn and master the technology and programming language they wan to master, including Git, GitHub, Jira, Figma, HTML, JavaScript, Node.js React.js, Python, Java, and so much more.
